Leads collected on 3 November 2026, the day monthly ESA CCI chlorophyll joined sea ice on the 4D page and the animation became watchable. Nothing here is promised. It is a list to reach for when there is time for the next wave. done marks what already works, open what is waiting on a question.

Where it stands

  • doneChlorophyll reads the 2160 by 1080 overview (chlor_a_x4) through the raad.virtualize index refs, like sea ice, instead of through the COG headers: about half the time, a third of the requests.
  • doneChunk reads that sit close together in one file go as one range request (zarrita's withRangeCoalescing through icechunk-js): a month of chlorophyll is 1 request, not 15. #coalesce=0 turns it off to compare.
  • doneRead-ahead steps through each layer's own next time steps, all layers at once. The Timing panel (Copy timings) says where each date's time went.
  • doneTime series at the picked point: every surface layer against time over 1 to 8 years, with the sea ice edge marked, and a line on when the ice fell through 15% and when the other layer peaked before it came back.

Retreat to bloom: when and where

The question the animation opened up: sea ice retreat seeds production, but when and where, and with what lag.

A lag map over the ice zone

The point series computes one lag (ice falls below its edge, chlorophyll peaks before the ice returns). Doing that for every cell of the seasonal ice zone, per season, gives a map of the lag, and a year by year stack of them. The pure parts are in src/core/series.js already (crossings, peakAfter, retreatToPeak); what a map needs is reads that run along time instead of across space (see time-chunked copies, below).

Time since melt

raadtools has a time-since-melt product (R/deriv.R, calc_time_since_melt). It stores no maps. It stores a table of transitions, one row per cell and date where the cell's state changed (columns idx, date, state: melted, open water never frozen, or frozen), on the NSIDC 25 km south polar stereographic grid (316 by 332). Any date's map is rebuilt from it: the last transition at or before the date gives days since melt, cells still frozen take the next melt after it, and a few sentinel codes mark land, open ocean and ice that never melted.

  • That table is small and sorted, so it travels well: exported as Parquet next to the other indexes, the browser could rebuild any date's map in a few milliseconds (a pure function in src/core), and a point's series is just that cell's rows.
  • As a layer it would sit with sea ice and chlorophyll on the same clock: time since melt is the x axis the bloom question wants.
  • openWhere the DuckDB transitions table is built: it comes off the back of the bowerbird and blueant data repository, but the code that builds it is not located yet.

Faster reads

  • Pawsey over S3. Pawsey Acacia also speaks the S3 API. Worth trying at the raad.virtualize level, with the store able to name either form for the same objects. Today Pawsey answers HTTP/1.1, so a browser keeps about six requests open to it, and each took about 1.3 s.
  • Time-chunked copies. A point series reads one whole chunk per time step (186 steps of two daily OISST layers took about 30 s). A copy chunked along time (for a region, or at overview resolution) would make series and lag maps a handful of reads.
  • Overview arrays. chlor_a_x2 to x32 carry no units attribute yet; the registry fills it in.
  • The COG header route (kept for comparison): open only the image a read needs, not every IFD, and ask for bigger blocks.
  • A mixed flat store. The overview build showed that jobs at the flat store level are cheap. That opens a Lance-backed flat store, with a generic fallback, holding materialised and virtual chunks side by side: one of Icechunk's big offers, with less bespoke tooling.

Voxels and chunk statistics

Added 4 November 2026. Prompted by the voxel map trend, and the Warsaw in Voxels demo (OpenStreetMap, lidar and a city tree database at 1 m voxels, drawn with Three.js; no code published). The look is not the point. The point is that an ocean model already is a voxel grid, and a voxel drawn at its true size shows the data as it is.

Model cells as blocks

  • BRAN cells (lon, lat, depth, time) drawn as blocks of their true extent, no interpolation: thresholded ("water warmer than 2 C", "salinity above 34.7") and cut away like the cutaway page. Picking a block gives its value and the chunk reference it came from.
  • Instanced boxes carry it to about 1e5 visible blocks. Past that, ray marching through a WebGL2 3D texture (one chunk or one overview level per texture) is the way to scale.

Chunks as the coarse voxel, with sufficient statistics

The trend.sst scheme, carried over to chunks. trend.sst stores, per S2 cell and date, n, sumw, sumwx and sumwx2. Sums are associative, so any union of cells over any dates aggregates exactly, and every mean, variance or rollup is arithmetic on the sums. For a 3D store, the native chunk is the natural cell.

  • One small table per variable: a row per (chunk, time step) with n, sumw, sumwx, sumwx2, plus min and max. Weights are cell area times layer thickness, so means are volume means. BRAN temp at 1x1x300x300 chunks is 51 by 5 by 12 chunks per day, about 3000 rows.
  • The browser reads that table first and draws the chunk grid as a blocky volume straight away (mean, variance, or fraction of cells past a threshold), then refines into real chunks only where the view or the threshold asks for them. min and max also prune: a chunk whose max is below the threshold is never read.
  • Any region and depth range over any dates is a selection of rows, so a volume mean series, a monthly climatology or an anomaly against it costs no chunk reads.
  • Trends: adding sumwt and sumwt2, sumwtx (t the time) gives the least squares slope per chunk exactly from the sums, aggregated over any span. A trend volume falls out of the same table.
  • Building it is a job at the flat store level (like the overview refs), next to the raad.virtualize indexes, written as Parquet. Each date is independent, so it backfills and resumes like trend.sst.
  • Decided (4 November): native chunks for now. They are free to build and match what the viewer reads. A grid shared across sources (the trend.sst S2 registry is the 2D version) is a different kind of step, for later.

Elsewhere in the mesh work

  • Greedy meshing (meshcore): merge neighbouring voxel faces into quads and drop the hidden ones, so a voxel block becomes an ordinary mesh with a CRS, renderable by rayvertex or rayrender. The 3D version of what quadmesh did for pixels.
  • Voxelised lidar (pointcore): counts, max height or class per voxel, a 3D raster of a point cloud and a level of detail for drone lidar. Prior art: lidR voxel_metrics, the PDAL voxel filters, Open3D voxel_down_sample. The same sufficient statistics apply per voxel.

The viewer

  • openThe relay URL for NSIDC (no CORS): stores.json lists the host, and the relay needs it on its list too.
  • Colour on the GPU: values as a float texture and the palette as a lookup in a shader, so painting leaves the main thread.
  • Series for ocean model layers at the level shown, and a CSV of any series.
  • Play by a layer's own step, so a monthly layer on a daily clock does not repeat its month.
